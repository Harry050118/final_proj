**Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation.** Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) enhances large language models by incorporating external knowledge to mitigate factual hallucinations and knowledge gaps [1]. Early RAG systems typically perform a single round of retrieval based on the initial query, which is often insufficient for complex questions that require multi-step reasoning [2]. To address this limitation, agentic RAG frameworks interleave reasoning with dynamic retrieval, allowing models to autonomously decide when and what to retrieve [3, 4]. Search-R1 trains LLMs to reason and leverage search engines through reinforcement learning, using outcome-based rewards and structured token delimiters to enable multi-turn search-reasoning loops [5]. R1-Searcher introduces a two-stage RL framework that first teaches retrieval invocation and then refines answer generation, demonstrating strong performance on multi-hop benchmarks without supervised fine-tuning [6]. R1-Searcher++ extends this approach with a memory mechanism and internal-external knowledge switching, reducing retrieval counts while improving accuracy [7]. However, these methods rely primarily on outcome-based rewards, which provide sparse, coarse-grained signals that may fail to correct suboptimal intermediate search behaviors.

**Process Reward and Fine-Grained Supervision.** Outcome-based reinforcement learning rewards models only for final correctness, offering limited guidance during multi-step reasoning [8]. Process reward models have emerged as a promising alternative, providing dense, step-level supervision to improve reasoning quality [9]. PRIME introduces implicit process reward modeling that derives dense rewards from outcome labels and policy rollouts, achieving substantial gains in reasoning benchmarks without expensive process annotation [10]. In the context of agentic search, VERITAS embeds faithfulness metrics into RL training to supervise intermediate reasoning steps, improving reasoning traceability while preserving task accuracy [11]. Search-P1 proposes path-centric reward shaping that evaluates entire reasoning trajectories with dual-track scoring and soft outcome credit, addressing sparse reward problems in agentic RAG [12]. These approaches demonstrate that fine-grained, process-level feedback can improve both stability and sample efficiency in RL-based reasoning systems.

**Search Efficiency and Suboptimal Retrieval Behaviors.** Agentic RAG systems often exhibit two pervasive inefficiencies: over-search, where redundant retrievals waste computation, and under-search, where necessary retrievals are omitted [13]. Previous work has attempted to improve efficiency through length-based penalties or confidence thresholds, but these heuristics risk oversimplifying the problem by suppressing necessary searches [13, 14]. AutoSearch introduces adaptive search depth through a self-answer mechanism that dynamically determines minimal sufficient retrieval steps [15]. DeepRAG models retrieval decisions as a Markov Decision Process with binary tree search over parametric versus external knowledge paths, reducing redundant retrieval through step-by-step query decomposition [16]. Investigating LLMs' factual knowledge boundaries has shown that models systematically misjudge when external knowledge is needed, highlighting the challenge of accurate retrieval decisions [17]. These findings motivate the need for explicit, step-level feedback on search necessity rather than coarse efficiency penalties.

**Multi-Hop and Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering.** The evaluation of agentic RAG systems relies on benchmarks spanning single-hop and multi-hop reasoning. HotpotQA introduces a large-scale Wikipedia-based dataset requiring reasoning across multiple documents, with sentence-level supporting facts for explainability [18]. MuSiQue constructs multi-hop questions via single-hop composition with strict filters to prevent shortcut-based solutions, exhibiting substantially higher human-machine gaps than existing benchmarks [19]. TriviaQA provides a large-scale reading comprehension dataset with complex, compositional questions requiring multi-sentence reasoning [20]. Natural Questions contains real user queries issued to Google Search, offering a realistic test of open-domain question answering [21]. Dense passage retrieval underpins modern RAG systems, with E5 demonstrating that weakly-supervised contrastive pre-training achieves strong zero-shot retrieval performance without labeled data [22]. Together, these datasets and retrieval methods provide the foundation for evaluating agentic search agents on diverse knowledge-intensive tasks.
