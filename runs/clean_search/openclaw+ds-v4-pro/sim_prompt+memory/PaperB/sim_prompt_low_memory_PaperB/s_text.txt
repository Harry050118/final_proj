**MLLMs as Evaluators.** Large language models have been widely adopted as judges of model outputs, with Zheng et al. [1] showing that strong LLMs can approximate human preferences at over 80% agreement. However, LLM-based evaluation exhibits systematic biases: Shi et al. [2] documented position bias across pairwise and listwise comparison settings. Pan et al. [3] trained VLM-based evaluators for digital agent trajectories and used them for inference-time refinement and behavior cloning, demonstrating that automatic evaluators can improve agent performance without additional supervision. Beyond outcome-based evaluation, Lightman et al. [4] showed that process supervision—providing step-level feedback—outperforms outcome supervision for training reliable reward models, motivating finer-grained approaches to verification.

**AI Agents and Verifier-Guided Improvement.** Realistic benchmarks have catalyzed progress in autonomous agents. WebArena [5] introduced a self-hosted web environment with 812 tasks spanning e-commerce and collaborative development. VisualWebArena [6] extended this to multimodal tasks requiring visual grounding, while OSWorld [7] provided a real computer environment spanning cross-application desktop tasks across operating systems. Shinn et al. [8] proposed Reflexion, where agents verbally reflect on task feedback to self-improve without weight updates. Koh et al. [9] introduced tree search for language model agents in web environments, using model-based value functions to guide exploration and demonstrating that test-time search substantially boosts success rates. Wang et al. [10] proposed Agent Workflow Memory, extracting reusable routines from agent trajectories to guide future task execution through continual learning.

**Test-Time Scaling and Reasoning.** Inference-time computation has emerged as a complementary axis for improving model capabilities. Wei et al. [11] introduced chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, demonstrating that intermediate reasoning steps significantly improve complex reasoning. Wang et al. [12] proposed self-consistency, which samples diverse reasoning paths and selects the most consistent answer via majority voting. More recently, DeepSeek-R1 [13] showed that large-scale reinforcement learning can elicit emergent reasoning behaviors including self-verification and reflection. For multimodal settings, Yang et al. [14] introduced Set-of-Mark prompting, overlaying visual marks on images to enhance the grounding capabilities of large multimodal models. Despite these advances, prior work has not systematically studied how agreement bias in MLLMs limits their effectiveness as verifiers of agent behavior in open-ended settings.
