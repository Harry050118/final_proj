**VLM-based Mobile GUI Agents.** VLM-based mobile agents have attracted considerable attention for their ability to interact with graphical user interfaces and autonomously perform daily tasks. Early work leveraged proprietary models such as GPT-4V for zero-shot smartphone GUI navigation, interpreting screen images and natural language instructions to generate executable actions [1]. CogAgent [2] introduced a specialized VLM with dual visual encoders for GUI understanding and navigation, while SeeClick [3] enhanced large vision-language models with GUI grounding pre-training for precise element localization across platforms. More recently, foundation action models including OS-Atlas [4] and UI-TARS [5] achieve strong performance through large-scale continual pretraining on multi-platform GUI screenshots. To improve reasoning quality, the Chain-of-Action-Thought (CoaT) paradigm [6] structures each mobile action into a multi-step thinking process—description, action-thought, decision, and grounding—yielding substantial improvements over single-step action prediction. However, supervised fine-tuning on fixed CoaT trajectories can cause agents to overfit to limited reasoning patterns, motivating approaches that go beyond static demonstrations.

**Preference Optimization and Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning.** To address SFT limitations, recent work explores reinforcement learning (RL) and preference optimization for improving model reasoning. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) [7] provides a lightweight alternative to RLHF by directly optimizing a policy from pairwise human preferences. In the reasoning domain, ReFT [8] applies PPO-based RL fine-tuning to chain-of-thought reasoning, using ground-truth final answers as rewards but overlooking intermediate step quality. ReST-MCTS* [9] infers process-level rewards through MCTS rollouts guided by oracle answers, though it requires access to ground-truth solutions and becomes expensive when scaled to visual domains. Xie et al. [10] employ MCTS to generate step-level preference signals for iterative DPO training, while Step-DPO [11] treats individual reasoning steps as stand-alone preference units. Tree-based methods such as TPO [12] and TreeRL [13] align models with multi-branch reasoning trees through preference ranking or on-policy RL with process-level supervision. 

For mobile GUI agents specifically, ReachAgent [14] applies reward-based preference learning with a multi-level reward function to compare action quality. TCPO [15] introduces thought-centric preference optimization with an Action Policy Consistency Constraint, which aims to align intermediate reasoning with final actions. However, existing approaches either depend on expensive process-level annotations, require online device interaction with slow trajectory collection, or operate at coarse granularity without explicitly optimizing intermediate reasoning steps. MobileIPL addresses these gaps by constructing a CoaT-tree through iterative sampling, scoring intermediate steps via rule-based rewards without manual annotation or learned process reward models, and performing thinking-level DPO with backward credit assignment—while its instruction evolution strategy prevents overfitting and expands the reasoning space.
