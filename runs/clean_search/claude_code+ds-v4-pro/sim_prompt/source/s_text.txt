**LLM-based Evaluation and Verification.** The use of large language models (LLMs) as evaluators has been established through the LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm [1], which demonstrated that strong LLMs can approximate human preferences in open-ended evaluation settings with over 80% agreement with human ratings, while also revealing systematic biases including position bias, verbosity preference, and self-enhancement tendencies. This paradigm has been extended to multimodal settings through MLLM-as-a-Judge frameworks [2], which assess model outputs across vision-language tasks via scoring, pair comparison, and batch ranking, revealing both the promise of MLLMs in evaluative roles and their susceptibility to inconsistencies and biases that mirror text-only judges. To improve the reliability of LLM-based evaluations, chain-of-thought prompting [3] elicits intermediate reasoning steps that enhance performance on complex tasks, while self-consistency [4] further improves reliability by sampling diverse reasoning paths and marginalizing over them to select the most consistent answer. Beyond using LLMs as judges of other models' outputs, generative verifiers [5] reframe reward modeling as next-token prediction, enabling LLMs to produce verification rationales before predicting correctness scores and unifying evaluation with language generation. In parallel, process reward models [6] supervise reasoning at the step level rather than only at the outcome, demonstrating that fine-grained, step-level feedback yields substantially more reliable rewards than outcome-only supervision in mathematical domains. Despite these advances, LLM-based evaluators remain subject to several forms of bias—including self-preference, where models favor their own outputs, and sensitivity to prompt phrasing and option ordering—which persist even when employing bias-mitigation strategies such as label shuffling and criteria rephrasing. These challenges are particularly salient in open-ended, multimodal domains where evaluation criteria are nuanced and difficult to formalize as precise rules, motivating the development of methods that can better leverage MLLMs' world knowledge and reasoning capabilities for verification.

**Autonomous Agents and Self-Improvement.** The development of language model agents capable of reasoning and acting in interactive environments has been advanced by frameworks such as ReAct [7], which interleaves reasoning traces with actions to enable grounded, dynamic decision-making through interaction with external environments. Building on such agent architectures, self-improvement methods have emerged that enable agents to refine their behavior from experience. Reflexion [8] introduced a verbal reinforcement framework where agents generate reflective textual summaries from environmental feedback, stored in episodic memory to guide future decisions without requiring model fine-tuning. In robotics, diffusion policies [9] have proven effective for learning visuomotor behaviors from demonstration data by framing action generation as a conditional denoising process, achieving strong performance on complex manipulation tasks. A critical dependency across these agent and self-improvement frameworks is the quality of the verifier or reward signal that provides feedback on agent behavior. When the reward function is unreliable or biased, self-improvement can plateau or even degrade performance. In web and computer-use domains, this dependency is particularly acute given the difficulty of defining hard-coded success criteria, motivating the use of learned or prompted verifiers to provide supervision signals for agent refinement. However, the quality of such verifier-provided feedback remains underexplored, especially for trajectories involving multimodal inputs and long-horizon tasks.

**Benchmarks for Multimodal Agent Evaluation.** Evaluating agents in realistic environments requires benchmarks that capture the complexity and multimodality of real-world tasks. WebArena [10] introduced a self-hosted web environment spanning e-commerce, social forums, software development, and content management domains, with 812 long-horizon tasks that require agents to navigate websites, manipulate content, and reason across modalities. VisualWebArena [11] extended this paradigm to visually grounded tasks, introducing 910 tasks across classifieds, shopping, and social media environments where agents must process image-based instructions and visually grounded web content, and proposed set-of-marks prompting to facilitate visual grounding. OSWorld [12] further broadened the scope by providing a real computer desktop environment supporting multi-application workflows across operating systems, with 369 open-ended tasks requiring both GUI and command-line interactions. In robotics, robomimic [13] provides long-horizon manipulation tasks and large-scale human demonstration datasets, enabling systematic evaluation of imitation learning and offline reinforcement learning methods on complex, multi-stage behaviors. Across these benchmarks, evaluation is typically performed by oracle verifiers—human-written scripts with privileged access to environment states—which, while correlated with human judgments, can be lenient or brittle due to hard-coded rules applied to limited state subsets. AgentRewardBench [14] recently introduced a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate the quality of LLM-based judges on web agent trajectories, comprising 1,302 expert-annotated trajectories across five environments and revealing that no single LLM consistently outperforms others across all evaluation dimensions. These efforts motivate the development of more flexible and reliable learned verifiers capable of nuanced, multimodal assessment of agent behavior while avoiding systematic biases that can impair their practical utility in downstream applications.
